Promotional Text

Description
Haiku looks simple - three lines, seventeen syllables - but the form most people learn in school skips its actual backbone: the kigo, a word or phrase that fixes the poem to a specific season and carries centuries of accumulated association. Without a kigo, a three-line poem is just a short poem, not a haiku in the classical sense. Haiku Muse teaches that distinction by building it into every poem it writes. Describe a moment or scene in your own words - a single sentence is enough. A cup of coffee going cold while you watch snow start. The first cicada of the summer. Our AI, composing in the classical 5-7-5 syllable structure, turns your moment into a real haiku and picks a genuine kigo appropriate to the scene from Japan's traditional seasonal-word almanacs (saijiki). It then explains, in plain language, which kigo it used, which season and sub-season it belongs to (early spring, midsummer, first frost, and so on), and why that word fit your moment. Each haiku also comes with a short note on the classical poets historically associated with that kigo or a closely related one - Matsuo Basho's frog and old pond, Yosa Buson's spring rain, Kobayashi Issa's small creatures - so a single generated poem becomes a doorway into real literary history rather than a novelty one-liner. The Muse Log tab saves every haiku you generate along with its kigo explanation, so your collection becomes a personal, season-tagged anthology over time. A limited number of free haiku are available each day, with the option to unlock more by watching a short rewarded ad.

Why This App

Most "haiku generators" produce any three short lines and call it done, skipping the seasonal-word convention that actually defines the form. Haiku Muse starts from an everyday moment and turns it into a real 5-7-5 poem built around a genuine kigo, with an explanation of the season and the poets tied to it - so a thirty-second app interaction leaves you with a small, real piece of Japanese literary tradition instead of a generic rhyme.
