Description
Chochin - the collapsible paper-and-bamboo lantern - has marked entrances, festivals, and family altars across Japan for centuries. But outside Japan, almost every lantern looks the same at a glance, and few people realize that shape, color, and the calligraphy painted on the paper all follow real conventions tied to a specific place or occasion. Describe the scene you're picturing in a sentence - a shrine festival at night, the red lantern outside a small izakaya, a family altar during the Obon season, a formal entrance at a ryokan - and our AI, playing the role of a chochin maker, matches you to the real style that scene calls for, from five genuine categories: yumihari-jochin (the bow-strung lantern carried by hand at festivals and patrols), takahari-jochin (the tall pole-mounted lantern marking shrine and shop entrances), goshintou (the shrine lantern signalling sacred ground), kanban-jochin (the classic red signboard lantern painted with a shop's name), and bon-jochin (the patterned lantern lit at home altars to welcome ancestral spirits each summer). Each match comes with a clear explanation of why that style fits the scene, the shape and coloring that distinguish it from the others, and a genuine detail about the calligraphy or pattern conventions painted on real examples - not generic craft trivia, but real practice rooted in the tradition. The Lantern Styles tab lets you browse all five categories as a standalone reference any time, even without asking for a match. Your past matches are saved to a personal history so you can revisit them next time a new scene comes to mind. A limited number of free matches are available each day, with the option to unlock more by watching a short ad.

Why This App

Most explanations of chochin treat every lantern as the same generic paper light, missing that yumihari, takahari, goshintou, kanban, and bon-jochin each carry a distinct shape, color, and purpose. Chochin Glow starts from a described scene and matches it to the correct lantern style with real conventions explained - turning a familiar sight into a small, practical piece of cultural knowledge.
