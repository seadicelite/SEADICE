Description
Every torii gate marks the same threshold - the boundary between the everyday world and sacred ground - but the gate itself is never generic. A shrine dedicated to a mountain kami, an Inari fox spirit, or an imperial ancestor each tends toward a different structural family, from the unadorned cylindrical pillars of a Shinmei-style gate to the upward-curving crossbeam of a Myojin-style gate to the double crossbeam of Kasuga style, and Inari shrines are famous for stringing hundreds of vermillion torii into a walkable tunnel. Torii Gate Guide turns a description of your shrine visit into a starting point for understanding what you're looking at. Tell it the deity or shrine type, the occasion (New Year visit, a specific festival, a casual stop while traveling), and the region, and the AI matches it to the torii family most associated with that context, explains the structural cues that distinguish it - straight versus curved crossbeams, the presence or absence of a plaque (gakuzuka), painted versus unpainted wood - and walks through the real etiquette of passing through: bowing once before entering, and walking along the edges of the path rather than straight down the center, which is traditionally left open for the kami.

Why This App

Most travel apps treat a torii gate as a photo backdrop rather than a structure with real regional and religious meaning. Torii Gate Guide is grounded in the actual classification of torii architecture and the etiquette that accompanies it, turning a shrine visit into a small, structured way to understand what makes each gate distinct.
