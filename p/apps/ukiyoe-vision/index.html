Description
Ukiyo-e - "pictures of the floating world" - are some of the most recognized images in art history: Hokusai's Great Wave, Hiroshige's rain-swept bridges, Utamaro's portraits of Edo-period women. Most people have seen one printed on a mug or a tote bag, but almost no one outside Japan knows how these prints were actually made, or which one resonates with a moment in their own life. Ukiyo-e Vision closes that gap. Describe a scene from your day or how you're feeling right now - a quiet rain outside your window, a crowd you pushed through on the way to work, a stillness before a decision - and our AI, working from a curated set of real, historically documented ukiyo-e prints, matches you to the single work that fits best. Each match comes with a clear explanation of why it was chosen, who made it and when, and what it actually depicts. Every match also includes a plain-language breakdown of the nishiki-e multi-block printing process behind it: how a single design was separated into a keyblock line drawing and multiple color blocks, how bokashi gradation was hand-brushed onto the woodblock before printing, and, for prints that use it, how mica dust (kirazuri) or blind-embossing (karazuri) added texture no single block of color could. The Prints tab lets you browse the full curated collection - Hokusai's Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, Hiroshige's Meisho Edo hyakkei, Utamaro's bijin-ga, Sharaku's kabuki actor portraits - as a standalone reference any time. Past matches are saved to a personal history so you can look back at how your days have been rendered.

About Ads

This app displays ads via Google AdMob, including a banner at the bottom of the screen. Free AI matches are limited to 5 per day; once that limit is reached, watching a rewarded video ad unlocks 3 additional matches. The scene or feeling you enter is sent to Anthropic's Claude API solely to generate your match and is not permanently stored on our servers.

Why This App

Ukiyo-e prints are everywhere in how the world pictures Japan, yet the actual prints, the artists behind them, and the woodblock process that made them possible stay largely invisible. Ukiyo-e Vision starts from something anyone can describe in a sentence - a moment from their day - and turns it into a real piece of art history: a specific, attributed print and an honest explanation of how it was physically made.
