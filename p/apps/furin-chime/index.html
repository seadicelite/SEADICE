Description
A furin usually reads as a small glass or metal bell that happens to tinkle in the breeze - pleasant, but interchangeable. Outside Japan, what's missing is that furin are not one object but several distinct crafts, and the material a furin is made from is not a style choice - it is the entire reason it sounds the way it does. Furin Chime closes that gap. Describe a mood, a memory, or a season, and our AI, working from documented furin craft tradition, matches your description to one of four real furin materials and the regional workshop tradition behind it - Edo furin, hand-blown glass from Tokyo with a deliberately unpolished, fire-cut rim (a smooth rim would let the internal glass clapper slide without friction and produce no sound at all) and designs hand-painted from the inside; Nanbu Tekki, cast-iron furin from the 900-year ironworking tradition of Iwate Prefecture, prized for a long, clear, slowly-fading tone recognized on Japan's official "100 Soundscapes Worth Preserving" list; Myochin Hibashi, a pair of hand-forged iron fire chopsticks reinvented as a wind chime in the 1960s by the 52nd generation of the Myochin family of armor-makers, ringing with the same bright, lingering tone as the tamahagane sword steel some pieces are forged from; and Takaoka furin, cast brass from the four-century-old copperware tradition of Takaoka City, Toyama, tuned for the brightest, most resonant pitch of the four. Each match explains the physical reason that material produces its particular tone, the regional craft history behind it, and the seasonal custom the sound belongs to: hanging a furin at a window or eave as an audible, low-tech signal that summer has arrived, long before electric cooling made the season bearable another way. The Craft Guide tab lets you browse all four materials, their tone characteristics, and their regional histories as a standalone reference at any time. Your past matches are saved to a personal Sound Journal so you can revisit which material matched which memory.

Why This App

Furin are sold everywhere as summer souvenirs but almost never explained. Furin Chime starts from something anyone can describe in a sentence - a mood, a memory, a season - and turns it into a real material, an honest explanation of why that material sounds the way it does, and the regional craft behind it, turning a familiar tinkling sound into a craft worth actually understanding.
