Promotional Text
Describe a trait, a goal, or a chapter of your life, and our AI matches it to a real historical samurai sword-guard (tsuba) design school and motif - the actual iron, gold, or shakudo work a real school produced - plus the documented symbolism and craft history behind that match.
Description
A tsuba is usually seen as decoration: the round metal guard on a samurai sword, sometimes framed and hung on a wall. Outside Japan, what almost never survives the photo is that a tsuba's school, metal, and motif were never arbitrary - they signaled the wearer's rank, patron, and temperament to anyone who knew how to read one. Tsuba Crest closes that gap. Describe yourself - a trait you carry, a goal you're chasing, a chapter you're in - and our AI, working from documented sword-fitting history, matches you to one of six real tsuba design schools, explains why that school's metalwork reads the way it does, and notes the kind of swordsman who traditionally carried it. Every match draws from real craft lineages: the Higo school, patronized by the Hosokawa clan, known for restrained plain iron and understated openwork (sukashi) that mirrored samurai austerity and discipline; the Owari school, whose thick iron plates carried bold, large-scale dragon and phoenix piercings favored by ambitious, forward-moving warriors; the Umetada school, Kyoto goldsmiths who inlaid chrysanthemum and paulownia crests in gold and silver onto iron, marking refinement and long-term mastery; the Nara school, whose high-relief carved pine and crane scenes (nara-bori) told a story in metal, suited to family- and legacy-driven paths; the Shoami school, a network of provincial smiths who adapted naturalistic bamboo and plum motifs region by region, matched to resilience through changing circumstance; and the Goto school, official fitting-makers to the Ashikaga and Tokugawa shogunates, whose shakudo and gold nunome-zogan work marked formal status and court standing. The Schools tab lets you browse all six design lineages as a standalone reference at any time, even without running a match. Your matches are saved to a personal history so you can revisit the school and motif behind who you were on a given day.

About Ads

This app displays ads via Google AdMob, including a banner at the bottom of the screen. Free AI matches are limited to 5 per day; once that limit is reached, watching a rewarded video ad unlocks 3 additional matches. The description you enter is sent to Anthropic's Claude API solely to generate your match and is not permanently stored on our servers.

Why This App

Tsuba look decorative but were built as a working signal system: metal, technique, and motif told a knowledgeable eye which school made it and what kind of warrior carried it. Tsuba Crest starts from something anyone can describe in a sentence - themselves - and turns it into a real matched design school, a real motif, and an honest explanation of the craft tradition behind both, turning a familiar sword-guard photo into a small, accurate piece of history.
