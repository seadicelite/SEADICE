Description
Karesansui, the "dry landscape" rock gardens found at Zen temples across Japan, are often photographed but rarely understood. Visitors see raked white gravel and a scatter of stones and assume the arrangement is decorative, when in fact every element follows centuries-old design rules: rocks are grouped in odd numbers to avoid visual symmetry, gravel is raked into specific patterns to suggest water around those "islands," and the whole composition is built around a principle called fukinsei - intentional asymmetry as a reflection of nature's own imperfection. Karesansui Zen turns those rules into something personal. Describe how you're feeling right now - overwhelmed, restless, quietly content, searching for clarity - and our AI, working from real karesansui design principles, generates a rock garden layout to match: how many stones, how they're grouped, what raking pattern surrounds them (straight lines, a swirl, concentric ripples), and a plain-language explanation of why that composition fits the mood you described. A separate Gardens tab lets you study real historic karesansui - Ryoan-ji's fifteen-stone garden in Kyoto, Daisen-in, Tofuku-ji - with notes on their actual documented design choices, so the app doubles as a small, accurate reference on a tradition most visitors only ever see in photos. Past generated layouts are saved so you can revisit how your mood has shifted over time.

Why This App

Karesansui gardens are one of the most photographed but least explained parts of a Japan trip - most visitors never learn that the arrangement in front of them follows real, deliberate design rules. Karesansui Zen starts from something anyone can name in a sentence - how they feel right now - and turns it into a garden built on those actual rules, so a moment of reflection comes with a real piece of Japanese design history attached to it.
