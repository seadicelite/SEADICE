Description
Suiseki, literally "water stone," is a centuries-old Japanese art of appreciating naturally formed stones as miniature landscapes - a distant mountain range, a plunging waterfall, a lone island, or a thatched hut, all suggested by nothing but the stone's unaltered shape, texture, and color. Unlike bonsai, a suiseki is never carved or reshaped; the entire discipline is about learning to see a landscape that was already there. Suiseki Stone turns any stone you have found - on a riverbed, a beach, a mountain trail - into a starting point for that discipline. Describe its rough shape and where you found it, and the AI matches it to one of the traditional suiseki classifications, explains whether a hand-carved wooden daiza stand or a sand-filled suiban tray is the traditional way to display it, and walks through real viewing etiquette: how to orient the stone's "face" (kao) toward the viewer and how display customs shift with the seasons.

Why This App

Most rock-identification apps tell you what mineral a stone is made of, not what it looks like - and suiseki is entirely about the latter: reading a distant mountain, a waterfall, or an island in a stone's unaltered shape. Suiseki Stone is grounded in the real classification system and display conventions of this centuries-old practice, turning a stone picked up on a walk into a small, structured exercise in traditional Japanese aesthetic appreciation.
