Description
A kanzashi is far more than a decorative hairpin. In the Edo period it doubled as a hidden weapon a woman could defend herself with, and by the late Edo and Meiji eras it had become a precise seasonal and social signal: the flower, color, and dangling ornaments a woman wore in her hair told onlookers the month, the occasion, and sometimes her exact status within an okiya. Kanzashi Bloom turns "what kanzashi should I wear" into a real, matched answer. Describe your occasion - a summer matsuri in a yukata, a coming-of-age ceremony (seijin shiki) in furisode, a tea ceremony, a wedding, or simply an everyday outing - along with your hair length and the color of your kimono or yukata, and our AI matches you to one of several real traditional styles: tsumami zaiku, hand-folded silk fabric flowers pieced together petal by petal, traditionally changed by season and famously worn by maiko, whose calendar runs from plum blossoms in January through cherry blossoms in April to maple leaves in November; bira-bira kanzashi, dangling metal ornaments hung with small fluttering strips that catch light and make a soft sound with each step, historically reserved for apprentice geiko; or a kushi and kogai set, a lacquered comb and long ornamental pin worn together in formal bridal and coming-of-age hairstyles such as the bunkin takashimada. Every match explains the motif in plain language - why a particular flower or color fits that month or occasion, and where the style historically comes from - plus exactly how it is traditionally positioned and worn in the hair, whether tucked into a bun, slid through a chignon, or clipped at the side. The Style Library tab lets you browse every kanzashi type and its seasonal motifs as a standalone reference at any time, even without running a match.

About Ads

This app displays ads via Google AdMob, including a banner at the bottom of the screen. Free AI matches are limited to 5 per day; once that limit is reached, watching a rewarded video ad unlocks 3 additional matches. The occasion and outfit details you enter are sent to Anthropic's Claude API solely to generate your match and are not permanently stored on our servers.

Why This App

Kanzashi guides online are usually either a single product photo with no context or a dense historical essay with no clear answer for what to actually wear today, leaving anyone dressing for an occasion unsure which of the many named styles even applies to their hair length or outfit. Kanzashi Bloom starts from something anyone can describe in a sentence - the occasion, their hair, and their outfit color - and turns it into a real matched style plus the seasonal reasoning and wearing guidance behind it, so a tradition that can feel opaque from the outside becomes an approachable, explained choice.
