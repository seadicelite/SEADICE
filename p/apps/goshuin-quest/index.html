Promotional Text
Describe a shrine or temple you visited - or plan to - and AI explains the real goshuin calligraphic seal-stamp tradition tied to it: how the stamp book is used, the etiquette for requesting a stamp, and what the calligraphy and vermilion seal imagery usually mean.
Description
Goshuin are handwritten in black ink and stamped in vermilion directly into a dedicated accordion-fold book, the goshuincho, and no two are quite alike - the calligrapher's hand, the date, and the seal design all change visit to visit. Tourists photograph finished pages constantly, but few understand what they're looking at or how to request one respectfully. Goshuin Quest closes that gap. Describe a shrine or temple - by name, by region, or just by what you remember about it - and our AI, working from documented shrine and temple practice, explains the goshuin tradition specifically tied to that place: what the central seal design usually depicts, what the calligraphy typically states (the deity or principal image enshrined, the temple or shrine name, the date), and any seasonal or limited-edition stamp variations the location is known for. The app also covers the etiquette that keeps the custom respectful: goshuin are traditionally received in exchange for a fee (typically 300-500 yen) at a dedicated stamp office (shuinjo) rather than requested casually at the main hall, a goshuincho should be purchased and used specifically for this purpose rather than any blank notebook, and stamps are understood as a record of worship rather than a mere souvenir - historically tied to the practice of hand-copying and offering sutras, for which the stamp was proof of the offering. Each entry also explains the difference between shrine (Shinto) and temple (Buddhist) goshuin conventions, since the two traditions use different seal formats and phrasing, and mixing them in a single stamp book is generally discouraged by many temples and shrines. The Journey Log tab lets you save every match and revisit the etiquette notes before your next visit.

Why This App

Goshuin pages are shared online constantly and understood rarely - most visitors know they're some kind of stamp but not that the design, calligraphy, and even the fee and etiquette are tied to a centuries-old sutra-offering practice. Goshuin Quest starts from a shrine or temple name and turns it into the real tradition and etiquette behind the page, so the stamp means something before it's collected.
