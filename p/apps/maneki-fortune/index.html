Promotional Text
Describe a wish for your shop, home, or new venture, and Claude AI matches it to the real maneki-neko (beckoning cat) it calls for - which paw is raised, which color, which pose - explaining the Edo-era folklore and shopkeeper etiquette behind that variant.
Description
Maneki-neko - the ceramic cat with one paw raised - sits by cash registers and front doors across Japan, but outside Japan it's usually read as one generic "lucky cat" figurine, with the actual differences in paw, color, and pose barely known. Maneki Fortune turns that generic reading into a specific, well-sourced match. Describe the wish behind your figurine in a sentence - more customers walking into a new cafe, steady income for a home business, protection and safety for a household, a friendship or romance you're hoping for - and our AI, playing the role of a maneki-neko shopkeeper, matches you to the real variant that wish calls for, from five genuine categories: migi-maneki (the right paw raised, the classic money-and-business-luck gesture), hidari-maneki (the left paw raised, associated with drawing in customers and people), kuro-maneki (the black cat, kept for protection against misfortune), momoiro-maneki (the pink cat, associated with love and relationships), and ryote-age (both paws raised, an all-around luck gesture more common in homes than shops). Each match comes with a clear explanation of why that variant fits your wish, the Edo-period Gotokuji temple legend most historians trace the figure back to, and a genuine shopkeeper etiquette note - which direction to face the cat, where to place it near an entrance or register - not generic gift-shop trivia, but real convention rooted in the practice. The Fortune Types tab lets you browse all five paw-and-color categories as a standalone reference any time, even without asking for a match. Your past matches are saved to a personal history so you can revisit them the next time a new wish comes to mind. A limited number of free matches are available each day, with the option to unlock more by watching a short ad.

Why This App

Most explanations of maneki-neko treat every figurine as the same generic lucky cat, missing that migi, hidari, kuro, momoiro, and ryote-age each carry a distinct paw, color, and purpose rooted in real shopkeeper convention. Maneki Fortune starts from a described wish and matches it to the correct variant with the folklore and etiquette explained - turning a familiar shop-counter object into a small, practical piece of cultural knowledge.
