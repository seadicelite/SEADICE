Description
Hina dolls - the ornate figures displayed every March for Hina Matsuri, Japan's Girls' Day - are often reduced abroad to "cute doll shelf." What rarely travels with the tradition is that there is no single "Hina doll set." There are real, distinct tiers, each historically built around specific court figures, and choosing the wrong one for your space or budget is the single most common regret among first-time buyers, whether in Japan or abroad. Hina Doll closes that gap. Describe your available space, your budget range, and whether you want a compact modern piece or a fuller traditional display, and our AI, working from documented Hina Matsuri tradition, matches you to the real tier that fits - from a single Emperor-and-Empress pair up to the full seven-tier display - and explains exactly who every figure in it is. Every match is grounded in real tiers: Shinno-kazari, the single-pair display of just the Emperor (Odairi-sama) and Empress (Ohina-sama), ideal for small apartments; Sandan-kazari, a three-tier display adding the Sannin-kanjo (three court ladies who serve the Empress); Godan-kazari, a five-tier display adding the Gonin-bayashi (five child musicians, each holding a real instrument - taiko drum, otsuzumi, kotsuzumi, fue flute, and utai singer); and the full Nanadan-kazari, a seven-tier display that further adds the Zuishin (two armed ministers, historically guarding the court on the left and right) and the Shicho (three servants, traditionally shown in comic expressions), plus tiers of miniature court furniture and a palanquin. Each match also explains real regional variation - most notably that Kyoto-style ("Kyo-bina") displays traditionally place the Emperor on the viewer's right, following old imperial court convention, while Kanto-style displays common in Tokyo place him on the left - and notes the difference between a full standing tiered display and the compact case-enclosed (kesu-iri) or storage-box (shumai-kazari) styles increasingly common in smaller modern homes. The Tiers tab lets you browse all display types and every figure's role as a standalone reference at any time, even without running a match. Your matches are saved to a personal history so you can revisit which display you chose for your home.

Why This App

Hina dolls are usually encountered abroad as a single generic image, stripped of the real tiers, named court figures, and regional conventions that make the tradition what it is - and first-time buyers regularly end up with a display that doesn't fit their space or budget simply because no single tier was ever explained to them. Hina Doll starts from something anyone can describe in a sentence - the room they have and the budget they're working with - and turns it into a real matched tier, a real explanation of who each figure is, and an honest note on regional variation, turning a seasonal decoration into a small, accurate piece of living tradition.
