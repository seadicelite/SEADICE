Description
A Daruma doll almost always travels as a souvenir photo: a round, red, faceless figure with two blank white ovals where its eyes should be. Outside Japan the object reads as generic good-luck decor, and the actual ritual it was built for - a real goal-setting practice still followed across Japan today - rarely comes with it. Daruma Goal closes that gap. Describe a goal you're working toward - a business target, an exam, a habit, a recovery - and our AI, working from documented Daruma tradition, matches your goal to one of the doll's real traditional colors (red for overall good fortune and protection from misfortune, gold for wealth and business success, green for health, purple for study and longevity, pink or white for love and relationships, and more), explains why that color fits, and walks you through the actual ritual: painting in the left eye the moment you commit to the goal, leaving the right eye blank as a visible, daily reminder of unfinished work, and painting the right eye only once the goal is achieved. Every match includes the origin story of Takasaki Daruma, the style produced in Takasaki, Gunma Prefecture, which has supplied the large majority of Japan's Daruma dolls for over two centuries, and a plain-language explanation of why the figure has no legs and always rights itself when tipped over - modeled on Bodhidharma, the monk credited with founding Zen Buddhism after years of unmoving seated meditation, and built with a weighted, rounded bottom so it always rocks back upright, the origin of the phrase "nanakorobi yaoki" (fall down seven times, get up eight). The Traditions tab lets you browse Daruma colors, regional styles, and the year-end Daruma Kuyo return-and-burn ceremony as a standalone reference at any time. Your goals, their matched color, and each eye-painting date are saved to a personal Goal Journal, turning a shelf ornament into an actual record of what you set out to do and when you finished it.
