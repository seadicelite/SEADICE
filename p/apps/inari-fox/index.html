Description
Inari shrines are the single most common type of Shinto shrine in Japan - more than 30,000 of them, ranging from Fushimi Inari Taisha's famous tunnel of thousands of vermilion torii gates down to a small inari-sha tucked behind a shop or in a rice field. What most visitors miss is that Inari worship isn't one thing: different shrines and different kitsune statues are dedicated to different blessings, and the fox messenger's pose and the object in its mouth - a key, a jewel, a sheaf of rice, or a scroll - each signal something specific. Inari Fox Guide turns your own wish into a way into that tradition. Describe what you're hoping for and the AI matches it to a real dedication style - business prosperity (shobai hanjo), a good harvest, household protection, or safe travel - and explains which fox-messenger symbol is associated with it and why, in the voice of a shrine guide rather than a search engine summary. Each match also walks through the etiquette that's easy to get wrong on a first visit: bowing once before passing through the torii, purifying your hands and mouth at the temizuya, and the correct rhythm at the offering hall - two bows, two claps, one bow - so a first-time visit to any Inari shrine, large or small, feels informed rather than uncertain.

Why This App

Most travel guides mention Fushimi Inari's torii tunnel as a photo spot without explaining that Inari worship spans over 30,000 shrines with different dedication styles and fox-messenger symbolism. Inari Fox Guide turns "what am I even hoping for here" into a specific dedication style, a specific symbol to look for, and the etiquette to use it respectfully.
