Description
A kadomatsu is far more than a festive centerpiece - it's a pair of pine-and-bamboo arrangements set at the entrance to welcome the New Year's kami (toshigami) into the home, and every part of it carries meaning: pine (matsu) for longevity, bamboo (take) for resilient, fast growth, and a touch of plum (ume) for steadfastness through winter. But outside Japan - and increasingly inside it - most people who see a kadomatsu have no idea why it looks the way it does, or that its shape actually changes by region. Kadomatsu Craft turns a few details about your home into a way to understand this tradition properly. Tell it your household size, how much entrance space you have, and your region, and the AI matches you to a fitting scale and style - from a full-size paired arrangement to a compact tabletop version - and explains real regional differences, like the diagonally cut bamboo (sogigiri) associated with the Kanto style versus the traditional flat-cut bamboo (zundogiri) still used in parts of Kansai. Just as important as the arrangement itself is the calendar around it. The app walks through the traditional display window (from around December 13, but never put up on December 29 or 31, both considered inauspicious), and the removal date marking the end of matsu-no-uchi, which is January 7 in most regions but January 15 in parts of Kansai - plus a reminder that a kadomatsu should be respectfully burned at a dondo-yaki bonfire rather than thrown in the regular trash.

Why This App

Most guides to Japanese New Year traditions mention the kadomatsu only in passing, without explaining the real regional variation in how it's built or the specific dates that govern when it goes up and comes down. Kadomatsu Craft is grounded in the actual conventions behind the decoration, turning a few details about your own home into a structured way to understand and correctly observe the tradition.
