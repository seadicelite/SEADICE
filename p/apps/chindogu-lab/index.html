Tell us the small, specific annoyance that bugs you every day, and our AI dreams up a whimsical Chindogu - an "un-useless" invention in the spirit of Kenji Kawakami's original 1990s movement - explaining which of the Ten Tenets of Chindogu it satisfies and why a genuine Chindogu can never be quite good enough to actually sell.
Description
Chindogu is a real Japanese art form invented by Kenji Kawakami in the 1980s: deliberately impractical inventions that solve a real, everyday annoyance while creating a new, usually larger annoyance of their own. A proper Chindogu is governed by the Ten Tenets Kawakami wrote down - a Chindogu must actually exist and be usable, cannot be sold for profit, cannot be patented, must not be created purely as a joke or as social commentary, and above all can never be so genuinely useful that anyone would seriously adopt it. Chindogu Lab turns any everyday annoyance you can describe - a sock that never matches its pair, a phone that face-plants at dinner, a commute where you can never find a free hand - into a fully realized Chindogu concept. The AI proposes a specific, absurd-but-plausible device, describes exactly how it works and what new problem it introduces, and explains which of the Ten Tenets it satisfies and why. The Tenet Library tab lets you read all ten tenets and Kawakami's original philosophy as a standalone reference at any time, even without generating anything. This is a novelty and creativity app, not a product-design tool - nothing generated is meant to be built, patented, or sold, in keeping with the Tenets themselves.

About Ads

This app displays ads via Google AdMob, including a banner at the bottom of the screen. Free AI generations are limited to 5 per day; once that limit is reached, watching a rewarded video ad unlocks 3 additional generations. The annoyance description you enter is sent to Anthropic's Claude API solely to generate your Chindogu and is not permanently stored on our servers.

Why This App

Most "invention idea generator" apps either take themselves too seriously, proposing things that sound like real product pitches, or produce generic random-noun-mashup jokes with no coherent logic behind them. Chindogu Lab does neither: it is grounded in a real, documented Japanese design philosophy with an actual rulebook, so every invention it proposes has to justify itself against the same Ten Tenets a real Chindogu inventor would use, turning a five-second annoyance into a small, structured piece of absurdist design thinking.
