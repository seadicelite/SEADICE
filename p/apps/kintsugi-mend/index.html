Description
Kintsugi - "golden joinery" - is the centuries-old Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with lacquer dusted in gold, silver, or platinum, treating the break itself as part of the object's history rather than a flaw to hide. Outside Japan, kintsugi is usually reduced to a single inspirational photo, and almost no one knows there are several distinct real repair techniques, each suited to a different kind of break. Kintsugi Mend turns that gap into a small, useful discovery. Describe what broke - a tea bowl dropped on tile, a plate cracked clean in half, a vase missing a chip - and our AI, playing the role of a kintsugi restorer, matches your break to the single best-fitting technique from a curated roster of six real methods: Hibi (hairline crack), Kake (chip repair), Yobitsugi (patched with a fragment from a different piece), Tomotsugi (rejoined original shards), Makie-e (decorative gold-dusted seam), and Kintsukuroi finishing. Each match comes with a plain-language explanation of why that technique fits your specific break, the traditional materials involved (urushi lacquer, gold/silver/platinum powder), and one genuine piece of context - a historical origin, a tea-ceremony custom, or a food-safety note distinguishing display-only repairs from food-safe ones. The Techniques tab lets you browse all six methods as a standalone reference any time, even without describing a break. Your past matches are saved to a personal history so you can revisit a repair plan later. A limited number of free matches are available each day, with the option to unlock more by watching a short ad.

About Ads

This app displays ads via Google AdMob, including a banner at the bottom of the screen. Free AI matches are limited to 5 per day; once that limit is reached, watching a rewarded video ad unlocks 3 additional matches. The break description you enter is sent to Anthropic's Claude API solely to generate your match and is not permanently stored on our servers.

Why This App

Kintsugi treats breakage as part of an object's history rather than something to hide - a distinctly Japanese answer to a universal moment: the thing you loved just broke. But outside Japan, that idea rarely goes further than a single quote on a poster. Kintsugi Mend starts from the question anyone can answer - what broke, and how - and turns it into a real, technique-specific answer you could actually act on, so the craft becomes something usable the next time something breaks, not just a nice idea.
