Description
Every July, thin strips of colored paper called tanzaku appear tied to bamboo branches across Japan, each one carrying someone's handwritten wish. Most people outside Japan - and plenty inside it - see the colorful bamboo without knowing that the five traditional tanzaku colors (goshiki: blue-green, red, yellow, white, and black or purple) aren't decorative choices. They come from the gogyo theory of five elements, and each color is traditionally meant for a different kind of wish. Tanabata Wish turns your own wish into a way to understand that system properly. Write what you're hoping for and pick a general category - love, health, study, family, or something else - and the AI matches it to the color that tradition actually assigns to it, explaining the elemental reasoning: blue-green for self-improvement and growth (wood), red for gratitude toward family (fire), yellow for trust and relationships (earth), white for rules and duty (metal), and black or purple for learning and study (water). The app also walks through the real legend behind the festival: Orihime, a weaver star, and Hikoboshi, a cowherd star, separated by the Milky Way and allowed to meet only once a year, on the seventh day of the seventh month - if the sky is clear enough for magpies to form a bridge. It also covers a genuine regional quirk: while July 7 is the nationally recognized date, the famous Sendai Tanabata Matsuri is held over a month later, from August 6 to 8, following the old lunar calendar.

Most explanations of Tanabata stop at "colorful paper on bamboo" without covering why the five colors exist or which one actually fits a given wish.
