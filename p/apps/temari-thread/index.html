Description
A temari usually reads as a decorative sphere of colorful thread - a nice photo, a folk-craft souvenir picked up in a gift shop. Outside Japan, what's missing is the fact that temari isn't random decoration: every geometric pattern wound around the ball is a named, documented stitch technique, and the ball itself began life as a children's toy stitched by mothers and grandmothers as a wish for a child's happiness and safe growth. Temari Thread closes that gap. Describe a feeling, an occasion, or a person you're making something for, and our AI, working from documented temari tradition, matches your description to one of the real stitch patterns still taught today - kiku, the chrysanthemum burst; asanoha, the hemp-leaf lattice associated with children's healthy growth; kikko, the interlocking tortoise-shell hexagon for longevity; matsukawabishi, the layered pine-bark diamond, and more - explains the kagari stitching technique behind it (dividing the sphere into equal sections with guide thread, then stitching the pattern thread by thread over the marked lines), and tells you which wish the pattern traditionally carries. Every match also includes the regional style behind it, most prominently Sanuki temari from Kagawa Prefecture, officially recognized as a national Traditional Craft, alongside regional thread-craft variations from Iyo (Ehime) and other regions, and the origin story of temari itself: cores originally wound from kemari kickball scraps or rice husks and wrapped in silk or cotton thread, evolving from a children's bouncing toy into an art form exchanged as a wish-filled handmade gift, especially at New Year and for a newborn's first year. The Traditions tab lets you browse stitch patterns, regional styles, and the history of temari as a standalone reference at any time. Your descriptions, their matched pattern, and the wish behind each one are saved to a personal Pattern Journal, turning a familiar decorative sphere into a keepsake worth actually understanding.
